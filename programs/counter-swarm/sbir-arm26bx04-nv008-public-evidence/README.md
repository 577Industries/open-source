# Counter-Swarm Surrogate Evidence (577 Industries Inc.)

Public data release accompanying 577 Industries' work on decentralized counter-swarm
targeting: preliminary results from an internally funded, synthetic surrogate simulation
study of target-deduplication policies for an outnumbered defending UAS swarm.

**Caveat, applying to every number in this repository:** On a synthetic, unclassified
clean-sensing counter-swarm surrogate; preliminary and not government-validated.

## What this is

A defending swarm of interceptor UAS protects a fixed site against a larger attacking
swarm. Each defender selects its own targets with no central tasking. The study compares
target-allocation policy families (greedy baseline, auction/market allocation, geometric
partitioning, and kinematic-inference deduplication, in which each platform infers
teammate intent from locally observed kinematics alone) in two communication modes:
active (peers share intent) and fully passive (zero communications). The metric is mean
residual enemy combat power: the percentage of the original attacking swarm still
combat-effective after all defenders are expended. Lower is better.

## Headline results (means with 95% CI, 30 trials per cell)

At parity (1:1), under fully passive, zero-communications conditions, deduplication
policies cut mean residual enemy combat power by more than 40% versus the
no-deduplication greedy baseline: auction 9.0 ± 1.7% and kinematic-inference 9.2 ± 1.6%
versus greedy 15.8 ± 2.1%. With active communications at parity, auction allocation
near-eliminated the attacking swarm: 1.0 ± 0.7% versus 25.0 ± 2.3% for greedy. At steep
asymmetry ratios with single-engagement defenders, every policy saturates at the
expenditure floor, so policy differences concentrate at near-parity ratios. On a
synthetic, unclassified clean-sensing counter-swarm surrogate; preliminary and not
government-validated.

## What is deliberately not here

The simulation engine, the targeting and deduplication policy implementations, the test
suite, and the regeneration scripts are proprietary and are not included. This is a data
release: the results are fixed-seed and were regenerated byte-identically (the SHA-256
above is stable across from-nothing reruns on the private toolchain).
