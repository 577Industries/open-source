**1,302 station-hours**.

That's how long 2D horizontal RTK positioning error exceeded the 2.5 cm planting threshold across 25 NGS CORS stations in the IA / IL / IN / OH corridor during the May 10-12, 2024 Gannon G5 superstorm — the largest geomagnetic storm in over two decades.

Most precision-ag operators heard "GPS went out" during peak planting season. The American Farm Bureau Federation documented equipment shutdowns lasting 12 to 48 hours. But until now, nobody had quantified what actually happened at the receiver level — not Kp index, not TEC map, but **the 2D error envelope a row-crop operator saw on the screen of a StarFire 6000**.

We just published the first reproducible quantification:

📊 Headline: 95th-percentile peak ~3.0 m. ~150× quiet-period baseline. Aggregate 1,302 station-hours above 2.5 cm across 25 stations.
🔬 Method: NGS CORS RINEX (175 real files cached), GFZ Potsdam Kp + Kyoto WDC Dst (NOAA SWPC's archive only goes back ~30 days — important footnote for any retrospective study), v1 documented climatological model.
🧪 Reproducibility: `make all`. Cold cache run ~10 min. 40 tests at 80% coverage.
📖 Transparent disclosure: v1 is climatological, not full SPP. v2 swaps in real pseudo-range solutions via the HELIOS connectors layer. The methodology doc spells out the boundary.

This is one of four public artifacts in HELIOS — 577 Industries' NASA SBIR Phase I program (subtopic SPWX.1.S26A) on calibrated, provenance-tracked space-weather decision intelligence. The other three: a JSON Schema RFC for feature-level provenance, a public space-weather data adapter library, and a Bayesian Model Averaging fusion-engine framework with pre-registered hold-out validation.

What's the operational takeaway? **Federal space-weather data exists. It's good. What's missing is the translation layer that converts Kp=9 into "your StarFire 6000 will not hold sub-3 cm RTK for the next four hours."** HELIOS builds that translation layer.
